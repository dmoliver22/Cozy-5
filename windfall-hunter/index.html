Moat types that count

Scoring rubric

Eight dimensions, zero to five. Unicorn bar: total 32 or more, no dimension under 3, at least one hiding reason named, and the moat is external.

Lead indicators

Built from the past gold rushes below. The most reliable one across every case: a quiet edit to the channel's own help or policy page, one to four months before any press. The strongest disqualifier: a store with no payment rail.

Past flagship moments

What the capability was, which channel routed demand, what the ranking rewarded, how long the window lasted, and what was visible before it opened.

What we are hunting

A Windfall Position is a spot on the board that keeps paying because of where it sits, not what you do from it. The moat lives in law, contracts, physics, geography, or history. A skilled competitor cannot out-work it. Neither could you have. You were there when the door shut.

The archetype we are actually hunting

The AI coloring book on Etsy, 2022. A new capability made the product free to produce. A marketplace already had buyers searching for it and a ranking that rewards whoever sells first. The people who knew the tool and the people who knew the marketplace never met. Artists who could have done it refused on principle. The idea fit in one sentence, took an afternoon, and the first in became the name of the category. That is the generational one. Everything in this ledger is measured against it.

Its signature, in order of importance:

  1. The magic is in the idea. One sentence. If it needs a deck, a credential, or a licence, it is not this.
  2. Something just became free. A capability drop in the last few months collapsed the cost of making a product that people have bought for decades.
  3. Demand is already queued in a channel. Buyers are searching a marketplace today. The ranking brings them to you. You do not sell; you list.
  4. Nobody has connected the two. Verified by counting listings, not by assuming. Two tribes that do not read each other's forums.
  5. Incumbents will not follow fast. They despise the tool, or they are embarrassed by the product, or they are slow.
  6. First becomes flagship. Reviews, rank, and the category name compound to the first entrant for years after the window closes.
  7. Zero effort, zero capital. If it takes more than a weekend to ship the first unit, the window is not what we think it is.
Regulatory positions, credentials, and rosters are the lesser cousins. They stay in the archive because they are real, but they do not get called unicorns. A unicorn here is a coloring-book moment, caught in its first months.

Perfect timing, defined

Timing is the gap between when something becomes possible and when it becomes obvious. Perfect timing is measurable: name the enabling change, date it, and show that the people who could exploit it have not noticed while the people who noticed cannot act.

A candidate must name all three: the external moat, the dated shift that opened the window, and the specific reason it is still unclaimed. Two of three is a lead. Three of three is vetting. Three of three plus the score bar is a unicorn.

Operating loop

  1. Check the archive first. Run the overlap check. If the territory is exhausted, do not dig. If it is fertile with a hanging thread, pull that thread instead.
  2. Claim a territory. Log it as digging with the vector you came in through, before researching. This is what prevents duplicate work across sessions.
  3. Follow the rabbit hole, log the turns. Unconventional turns are the point: read the regulation itself, the procurement award, the obituary, the shutdown notice, the standards appendix. Record each twist in the territory notes.
  4. Log the verdict even when it is nothing. An exhausted territory with a dead-end reason is a result. It saves the next hunter a day.
  5. Score candidates coldly. Fill every dimension. If any is under 3, say why in the kill reason or the next action. Never round up.
  6. Leave a hanging thread. Every territory ends with the next thread to pull, or an explicit "none".
  7. Journal the cycle. What was tried, what moved, what the gut says next.

The standard

We are looking for the best of everything, not the best of what a search happened to turn up. A mediocre find logged as a candidate is worse than an honest dead end, because it pulls attention from the hunt. If a path is wrong, stop walking it. Do not keep following a vector to have something to show. Log the territory as exhausted, write down why, and turn.

Every record can be challenged with a button. The challenge is not an attempt to fail it. It is a second pair of eyes on the same side of the table: what is the strongest objection, what evidence is missing, what would change the verdict, and what new path does the objection open. A challenge that reopens a dead end or spawns a new territory has done its job as much as one that confirms a unicorn.

Kill rules

  • The moat is a skill, a relationship, or hustle. Kill it. That is an earned advantage, not a windfall.
  • The window cannot be dated to a specific change. Kill it or send it back to lead.
  • You cannot articulate why it is unclaimed. Assume it is claimed.
  • It needs meaningful capital or a licence you cannot get within weeks. Kill it unless the licence itself is the windfall.
  • Three funded competitors or a "best tools for X" listicle exists. The window is closing. Kill it.
  • It is only here because it was the best thing the search found. That is not a reason. Kill it or send it back to lead with a named evidence gap.
  • The position is a state-listed person and the listing costs under 40 hours and 30 days to get. A cheap, fast credential fills the list in weeks. Record credential cost and latency before scoring moat.

Lessons the archive has paid for

  • A thin list is not a thin market. Count students, not instructors. Colorado minted 330 instructors against 502 students in month one. Read the regulator's throughput numbers before believing a roster gap.
  • Portability kills geography. If the buyer can use any listed provider in the state, an empty county is a map of low demand, not unclaimed rent.
  • Look upstream of the mandate. The scarce thing in the Colorado cluster was not the new course but the old volunteer hunter-ed class it leaned on. New mandates bolted onto under-sized legacy programs create accidental bottlenecks.
  • Tech shutdowns are watched. Every SaaS death with scale had three funded "migrate from X" pages within a week. Orphans worth having are physical, low status, and below the incumbents' minimums.
