# Windfall Hunter: routines for a fresh session

You are continuing a hunt for "Windfall Positions": ideas where being first is the whole trick. The model case is the first AI coloring books on Etsy in 2022. Something just made a product cheap, buyers were already searching in a store with a ranking, nobody had connected the two, it took a weekend and no money, and the first one in became the name of the category.

## How routine sessions get this file

Scheduled routines run in fresh cloud sessions that have no copy of this repository and no GitHub access. This file is therefore published as a supporting file of the ledger artifact (path `ROUTINES.md` at https://claude.ai/artifact/VqggLy8ehMvboWRgiE8vKH); routine sessions read it with the Artifact tool (`action: read`, `path: ROUTINES.md`). Whenever this file changes, republish the artifact with `files: {"ROUTINES.md": ...}` so the routines see the new version, then commit. Routine sessions never edit or republish the page and never push to git; a rule or lesson they want on the page goes into a `journal` entry of kind `insight`, and the owner's main session carries it over.

## The bar

An idea is a unicorn only if: "Are you first?" is 4 or 5 by a count you ran (two kinds of first count: nobody has made it, or the shelf is busy but nobody on it uses the new machine yet; the 2022 coloring books were the second kind, sold on a full shelf, and that kind is where the big ones were); "Did the door just open?" is 3 or more with a date; the total is 30 or more out of 40; a reason is named for why nobody has done it; and it survived a challenge. Count on the surface where that market's builders actually publish (store pages, help centers, newsrooms, hobby blogs, Reddit), not GitHub alone. A new kit is not a new store. Stars are not shoppers. If the honest answer is "no", the affiliate rail is fake. First at a free reference page is not first on a shelf. Anything with a beta, leak cycle or pre-order period (Apple software and phones, consoles, annual tax forms) is pre-filled before launch day, so "first" is structurally unavailable there; mirror sites (Goodreads, ThriftBooks, AbeBooks) do not index new Amazon titles, so count Amazon snippets or ask a person. If the platform already hands out a free share link that does the job, a printable around it is decoration; find the oldest comparable listing and read its review count before scoring.

## Routine A: Monday early-warning scan and clock check

0. Read the `counts` collection first. Any count marked `done` carries a `result` written by a person with store access; treat it as the best evidence in the ledger and update the related candidate or territory before anything else. Any count still `open` past its `due` date: note it in the journal, do not silently drop it.

1. Read the `signals` collection (19 early warnings, each with a `now` field saying what was firing last time).
2. Run the forward scan with a subagent: new repos since the last scan in the GitHub orgs of Meta (meta-models, facebookincubator, facebookresearch), OpenAI, Anthropic, Google (google, google-gemini, google-deepmind), Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, NVIDIA, xAI, Mistral, Perplexity, Stripe, Shopify, Cloudflare, ByteDance, Tencent, Alibaba, DeepSeek, Moonshot; repos created in the last 30 days with more than 3,000 stars and what they prove for a normal person; first-port latency and derivative counts for any official kit; every "non-commercial", "no selling" or device-cap clause in kit terms; any new "does it work here" status page for consumer agents. Also scan headlines (The Information, Bloomberg, Reuters, TechCrunch, TestingCatalog) for code names, earnings-call category language, insider security leaks ahead of a launch, and app-store listings under a big company's developer id. Routine sessions usually get a 403 from GitHub search and cannot open many sites (darkvisitors.com, rankhero.com, kdp.amazon.com, faire.com, teacherspayteachers.com). If GitHub is blocked, fall back to GitHub's trending page and WebSearch in extended mode, and write 'partial scan' in the journal entry rather than skipping silently. Mark any score built only on search snippets as 'snippet only' in the evidence field.
3. Update each signal's `now` field with what is firing today and the date, in plain English. Correct anything last week got wrong.
4. Read the `candidates` collection. For every idea with a `deadline` on or before next Monday, run a challenge subagent (strongest objection, what would flip the verdict, counts on the right surface) and apply the result: drop it with the reason in `kill`, or keep it with a new deadline and a new `next`. Never let a deadline pass silently.
5. Read the `shelves` collection (watch especially for a shelf that just gained a price field, a new category or a new country; that is the emptiest a shelf ever is). For each shelf, check its `watch_url` and newsroom for a new category, format or rule change since `newest_change_date`. Update the shelf. If a new empty cell appears (a new shelf section with a price field and under about 50 entries), file it as a candidate with status `lead` and a count-first `next`.
5b. Etsy market-page scan. Etsy auto-creates a public page at etsy.com/market/<term> once a search term has enough buyers, so a page that exists with thin or stale listings is proof of demand without supply. Take 6 to 8 terms tied to events of the last 30 days (new devices, new laws, new programs, gift occasions), search them in extended mode (standard mode rarely returns Etsy pages), and read the snippet: listing titles, prices, seller names, whether listings predate the event. Use rankhero.com/keywords/<term> as a search-volume proxy. A page that exists with listings that all predate the event, or none that name it, is a candidate (status lead, count-first next). Re-check each term two weeks after its event; sellers move within about ten days.
5d. New-machine-on-a-full-shelf scan. List tools, models and features released or materially improved in the last 90 days that make some product much cheaper to produce. For each, name the busy shelf in the registry where that product is still made slowly by hand and buyers already pay, check the shelf's policy on machine-made items, and count whether any seller there uses the machine yet (new sellers since release, price collapse, tell-tale wording). A busy shelf with no machine-made listings is a candidate only if three more checks pass: (1) the machine does the step that eats the hours today, not an easy step beside it (write down which step is the bottleneck); (2) the machine's own maker does not already sell the finished product as a button; (3) the gatekeepers (distributors, store rules) accept the exact product, including the AI label. The flood tells are a halved price and the tool vendor marketing to that shelf's sellers; seeing both means first is 2 at most.

5c. Surprise-born shelf scan. The shelves where first is possible were born from events nobody could pre-produce for: a rule that took effect with short notice, a sudden platform policy (like the KDP cap), a recall, an outage, a product launched without an announcement, a free tier ending, a program that opened to the public with no beta. List the surprise events of the last 30 days from news snippets, and for each ask what object with a price field a buyer now needs that did not exist before, and on which shelf in the registry it would sit. Count that shelf directly. Cross off anything with a beta or pre-order period before counting.

6. Write one `journal` entry of kind `cycle` titled "Monday scan, <date>": what fired, what changed on shelves, what was challenged, what was dropped. Plain English.
7. If anything cleared the bar, say so in the final message so the owner is notified.

## Routine B: fortnightly wave map

1. Find the two or three things tens of millions of ordinary people got, started doing, or were hit by in the last 60 days. Verify each with a number and a date. Exclude waves already in the `waves` collection unless re-mapping them.
2. For each: list five things people are visibly doing in week three or four (Reddit, X, TikTok, press, forums). For each behavior, name the thing nobody has made: the "which one should I buy" guide, the "which companies accept it" table, the "what went wrong" log, the kit, the accessory, the per-store status page. Count that it is missing, with the query and the result. Check that people search for it and that there is a way to get paid on a shelf with a price field. Say who will make the official version and when.
3. Re-map every wave in `waves` with `live: true`: add the new week's behaviors, mark objects that got made by someone else.
4. File anything that scores first 4 or more and has a rail as a candidate (status `lead`, deadline inside two weeks). File each mapped wave as a `waves` document and the sweep as an exhausted `territories` document listing what was checked and set aside, so nothing is dug twice.
4b. Geographic-lag sweep (once a month, on the 1st). The English shelves flood first; the same machine-made products can be years behind on non-English shelves because the sellers there do not read English AI news. Pick three proven machine-made products (coloring and activity books, digital planners, AI-narrated audiobooks, printable worksheets, Yoto and Tonies audio) and count their adoption on three non-English shelves (Etsy as seen from DE, FR, JP, BR, PL; Amazon KDP .de, .fr, .co.jp, .com.br, .pl; Bol.com, Hotmart, eduki, Creative-Tonies, Kobo in Portuguese or Japanese). Check the payment rail for a US or UK solo seller and the platform's rule on foreign sellers. Being first in a market is a real first; a native-speaker check is the usual hidden cost.

4c. New-price-field sweep (every run). List every platform surface that opened, expanded to new creator groups or countries, or changed rules to allow a new kind of paid item in the last 60 days (platform newsrooms and creator trade press). A shelf is emptiest the day it gets a price field. For each: who can sell, how many items are on it today, whether a solo seller gets paid, the AI policy, and which proven object from an older shelf could be moved onto it in a weekend.

5. Write a `journal` entry of kind `cycle` titled "Wave map, <date>".

## Arbitrage (both routines)

Every idea has a `lane`: `first` or `arbitrage`. Arbitrage ideas have their own bar (see the "How this works" tab): the gap must be measured today on both sides after every fee, the door score 3 or more, easy-to-get-paid and costs-nothing 4 or 5, total 28 or more, a reason named for why the gap is open, and the closing mechanism written down. Anything outside a platform's rules, a law or a licence is dropped as a ban. Arbitrage needs a buyer on the other side: a fee saved on your own sales is housekeeping, logged as a lead, never called the find.

- Routine A (Monday): re-measure every live arbitrage idea on both sides. If the gap has closed, drop it with the date and what closed it. Record the new net gap in `evidence`.
- Routine B (fortnightly): run one arbitrage hunter alongside the wave map, covering royalty gaps between shelves, country price gaps on legitimately resellable goods, fee-change windows, points and credits, clearance and retirement, platform-to-platform spreads, and payout or currency gaps. At most four gaps, each measured with fees and inside the rules.

## Win-win-win (both routines)

The third lane: three parties each gain (the buyer, a partner or platform, you) and the other two bring people to you. Bar on the "How this works" tab. The cleanest form is a platform paying for growth during a launch window (referral bounties, zero-fee periods, creator funds, seller incentives) with an end date; record the end date as the window. Routine A: re-check every live win-win-win idea's end date and whether the other parties are still promoting it. Routine B: run one win-win-win hunter alongside the wave map, limited to incentives that STARTED in the last 60 days, pay a cash or cash-like 'you' leg, and allow public sharing (read the personal-use clause first). Standing referral programs are logged once and never re-dug.

## Lane lock

Never two hunts in a row in the same lane. Read the last two `journal` entries before choosing where to look.
